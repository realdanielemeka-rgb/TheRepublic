    What’s Your Chivita?

    Nigerian Marketing Awards 2024Winner · Best Use of Social MediaOfficial results · listed as the Chivita 2.0 Campaign (opens in a new tab) ↗

    The line
    Everyone has a Chivita. What’s yours?
    The Republic's role
    Organic social strategy · Creator partnerships · Always-on content · A YouTube series · Live social
    Partners
    The Republic × CHI Limited. Hosts and creators under Credits.
    Span
    One year in four chapters: creator stories, always-on content, Style N’ Sips and 12 Days of Christmas.

    01 · The problem

    Chivita is a familiar name in Nigerian homes, and familiar is not the same as talked about. The brand needed more engagement and visibility on social, and it needed to hold that attention for a year, not a launch week.

    02 · The human truth

    Everyone has a Chivita story.

    A drink after a long day, a carton on the family table, a memory with friends. The brand did not need to tell people what it meant to them. It needed to ask.

    03 · The idea

    Ask one question, then let everyone answer it.

    What’s your Chivita? Creators would answer first, in their own voice, and hand the question to their followers. The brand would keep the conversation going all year: in the feed, in a series people came back for, and live at Christmas.

    04 · The work

    1. Creator stories. Nine creators built Chivita into a real moment of their own day, then ended each video with the question for their followers: #WhatsYourChivita.
    2. Always on. The hashtag became a stream. Twelve creators returned with content on the campaign’s themes, people shared their own moments, and the brand answered in the feed. Six short promoted films carried the line to a wider audience: good times with friends, mums, the daily hustle, picking yourself back up, and love.
    3. Style N’ Sips. A YouTube series of style, humour and real talk, hosted by Elozonam, Akin Faminu and Jay On Air: eleven episodes, from Boys Will Be Boys to Movie Nights, each shared on Instagram and Facebook too.
    4. The season. 12 Days of Christmas: an Instagram Live Grotto Fiesta hosted by Jay On Air, with guest creators, games and giveaways, product packs for early viewers, hampers to win and a Christmas challenge for everyone watching.

    Spruce by Dulux · CAP Plc · 2026

    Show Your True Colours

    The line
    Show Your True Colours.
    The Republic's role
    Digital strategy · Creator and clipper distribution · Pinterest room inspiration · Campaign amplification
    Partners
    Concept and colour identities by 1879 Tech Hub / UACN. Films by KellyStropHQ. Full credits below.
    Launch
    Teasers through March and early April. Hero film published 6 April 2026; character films 7–11 April.

    01 · The problem

    Liking a colour is only the first decision. Before anyone buys paint, they ask whether it will feel like them, whether it will work in their space and which shade to choose. The launch needed digital work that helped people consider the paint, not only notice it.

    02 · The human truth

    People recognise an attitude before they choose a shade.

    03 · The idea

    Put the personalities into conversation, then into rooms people could picture as their own.

    The platform, ten colours as ten recognisable personalities, was created by 1879 Tech Hub / UACN. Our job was the digital route from noticing a colour to choosing one.

    04 · The work

    1. Question. Teasers asked what a colour is allowed to mean, before the range appeared.
    2. Reveal. The hero film and ten character films introduced the cast.
    3. Debate. Live creator pairings put personalities head to head: Steeze White meets Baddie Pink, Old Money Green meets Odogwu Red.
    4. Distribute. A ten-clipper network cut the debates into many edits. Paint became the hook; banter became the edit.
    5. Imagine. AI room inspiration on Pinterest placed the palette in spaces Nigerians could imagine living in, with the profile linking to the Spruce website.
    6. Choose. The route led to the Spruce website, which lets visitors drag to repaint a room, take a six-question quiz and carry the shade into the catalogue.

    Prudential Zenith Life · 2025–26

    Empowering Tomorrow

    The line
    Your next 40 years start today. Boss in 2026, boss in 2066.
    The Republic's role
    Campaign strategy · Content and social · Integrated communications
    Partners
    The Republic × Prudential Zenith Life. Execution credits under Credits.
    Span
    September 2025 to April 2026: a family film, a live CEO conversation, a pledge, a future-self film, billboards, radio and a website.

    01 · The problem

    Life insurance asks people to plan for a future that feels far away, while today is already full. Protection had to feel relevant while life is still being built, and people needed a clear place to start.

    02 · The human truth

    The future is a difficult family conversation.

    03 · The idea

    Make tomorrow personal. Make planning practical.

    A 40-year plan gave insurance a human horizon: a family to care for, a business to grow, a child's education, a later life with choices. Each chapter answered a different hesitation.

    04 · The work

    1. Talk. Oba and his dad made money and protection a family conversation. October 2025.
    2. Invite. Creator Olushola invited his audience to a live conversation with the CEO. November 2025.
    3. Ask. Viewers put their hesitations to the CEO directly, from affordability to what makes the company different. The full conversation stayed available as a podcast. December 2025.
    4. Pledge. It ended with a commitment people could repeat, “From today, I'm planning…”, returned to the feed as a short film. January 2026.
    5. Picture. A 49-second future-self film, made with GPT and Kling, named the ambitions: a bakery, the children's education, care for Mum. “Boss in 2026, boss in 2066” travelled through billboard creative and radio. April 2026.
    6. Start. The website turns the idea into a Ready Test, 40-year planning calculators and an advisor handoff.

    The Dispatch · Point of view and films

    Journal.

    Article · Point of view

    Daniel Emeka · Managing Director2 min read

    Write for the reply.

    Good brand work gives people a reason to bring their own lives into the story.

    Too much brand content is built to be looked at and left behind. It can be beautifully made. The frame is right, the product is in focus, the caption says something agreeable. Then it reaches somebody’s feed and asks nothing of them. They have seen a version of that scene before. They keep moving.

    When we begin a piece of work, I want us to ask a harder question: what is the person on the other side meant to do with it? Can they recognise something specific from their own life? Can they answer in a way that sounds like them? If the only possible response is to admire the execution, we have probably stopped too early.

    If someone offers you a piece of their story, the least you can do is show that you heard it.

    That changes the way we develop an idea. We look for the detail that makes a broad subject personal. We make room for disagreement, memory and interpretation. We give the audience a clear invitation, then decide what the brand will do with the answer. A question in a caption is easy to add. Building a response into the work takes more thought. The format, the follow-up and the brand’s voice all have to support it.

    With Onga, we asked, “What does home mean to you?” The question left space for people to bring their own memories of food and belonging. Their answers came back into the feed. That return matters. If someone offers you a piece of their story, the least you can do is show that you heard it.

    For Cowbell, the thought was the person behind your first taste. Around Ramadan, the first meal carries work that is often quiet: somebody prepares, remembers, makes sure everyone is ready. Recognising that person gave people a way to speak about care in their own homes. The brand had a role in the conversation because it had first paid attention to the people in it.

    This is how I want us to judge our work before it leaves the room. Who could answer it? What would their answer reveal? Would we know what to do next if they did? If those questions have no convincing answer, we keep working. Creating tomorrow begins with giving somebody a reason to take part today.

    Prudential Zenith Life

    You Matter

    A strategy campaign to make insurance feel human again.

    Nigerian Marketing Awards 2025Second place · Financial Institution of the YearOfficial results (opens in a new tab) ↗

    Client
    Prudential Zenith Life Insurance
    Sector
    Insurance & Finance
    Work
    Strategy · TVC · Out of home · Radio · Social · Search

    01 · The story

    Insurance is supposed to be personal, but in Nigeria it rarely feels that way. People don’t wake up thinking about cover. They think about school fees, rent and the future they haven’t fully figured out.

    The brief was to relaunch Prudential Zenith with a renewed identity and message, and to build awareness, reach and qualified leads. The challenge: remind people that planning ahead is an act of love, and make a financial product feel like an emotional decision.

    02 · The work

    1. A strategic reset, not just a campaign. This was about relevance, not visibility.
    2. A perspective, not a product. You Matter: Prudential Zenith’s promise to put its customers’ well-being first. Not a tagline, but a truth that cuts across media, culture and behaviour.
    3. The film. A TV commercial we took from pre-production to post: one family through the milestones they build together, from a dinner for two to a traditional wedding, a baby shower and a daughter’s birthday.
    4. Out of home. Supporting the life you’re building together: the family and the line on large-format billboards.
    5. Radio, social and search. A radio spot written and produced for national airwaves, customer testimonial content on social, and Google search and display ads that turned attention into visits and leads.
    6. Recognition. Second place for Financial Institution of the Year at the Nigerian Marketing Awards 2025.
